FP&A review and forecasting

Review a control book in seconds,
then see where the P&L is heading.

Clarity recalculates every formula in a quarterly control book, compares it to five years of history, flags what doesn't add up, and writes slide-ready commentary for the biggest movers. A second tab forecasts the P&L up to eight quarters out using five backtested models.

Run the Check tool Open the Forecast tool

All data is synthetic. Meridian Systems is a fictional company with three business units and FY22 to FY26 history.

01

Check

Upload a control book, or load a preloaded one, and click Analyze.

  • Recalculates every formula and ties out GM, Operating Income and totals
  • Flags broken references, inconsistent formulas, typed-over values and double counting
  • Machine-learning anomaly detection against FY22 to FY26 history
  • Ranks the top 10 movers and drafts slide-ready commentary

Forecast

Pick a business unit, the guiding principles, and a horizon of 1 to 8 quarters.

  • Five models: Holt-Winters, linear regression, SARIMA, seasonal naive and Theta
  • Backtests each on the last four quarters, then picks the winner or averages the best three
  • Forecasts drivers, then calculates GM and Operating Income so the P&L ties
  • Tests the forecast year against budget targets

How the Check tool works

  1. ReadFormulas and displayed values, read separately
  2. RecalculateEvery formula through Clarity's own evaluator
  3. AuditPattern, hardcode, duplicate and tie-out checks
  4. CompareRobust z-scores plus an isolation forest on 5 years of history
  5. ExplainBlend-ranked movers and slide-style commentary

Automate the labor, never the judgment

All of the math is done in code. The language model, when one is connected, only writes the commentary and never calculates a number. Without an API key, commentary falls back to deterministic templates.

Built from a real internship project

This demo rebuilds a tool designed during a Corporate FP&A internship. The original ran on internal data, so this version recreates the approach on an invented company that anyone can open and test.
